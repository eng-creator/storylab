About the project

Research idea

This Grade 5 pilot study explores whether pictures and emojis help children understand the main events, characters, actions, emotions, and sequence of short stories.

Main research question: Can Grade 5 students understand a story using pictures and emojis?

Aim

To investigate how well Grade 5 students understand short stories presented in different visual and textual formats.

Hypothesis

Pictures and emojis can help children understand key events and emotions, but some details may be harder to understand without written language.

Objectives

  • Explore visual storytelling through words, pictures, and emojis.
  • Compare student comprehension across three formats.
  • Identify which information is easiest or hardest to understand.
  • Collect and analyse pilot data.
  • Develop an interactive educational website based on the findings.

Research methods

1. Review

Explore basic ideas about visual communication and story comprehension.

2. Pilot experiment

Students complete 30 comprehension tasks across three formats.

3. Reflection

Students answer 5 questions about difficulty and preference.

Analysis: scores are converted into counts and percentages; format and category performance can then be compared.

Text story

The Missing Library Book

Grade 5Text only

Mira borrowed a blue adventure book from the school library on Monday.

She put the book in her backpack and went to art class.

After school, she walked home. At home, she opened her backpack, but the book was not there.

Mira checked her bedroom desk and then looked in the kitchen. She still could not find it.

Then she remembered that she had read the last two pages while sitting on a bench near the school garden.

The next morning, Mira returned to school and checked the bench. The blue book was under it.

She took the book back to the library. The librarian thanked her, and Mira felt relieved.

How do visual stories work?

Pictures and emojis can communicate parts of a story, but they do not always communicate every detail in the same way that sentences do.

👤

Characters

Look for people, animals, or objects that act in the story.

📍

Setting

Notice where the story happens. A place can be shown through visual clues.

➡️

Actions

Look at movement, changes, and the order of events.

❤️

Emotions

Faces, body language, colours, and symbols may suggest how a character feels.

🧩

Sequence

Put events in order: first, next, then, and finally.

💭

Inference

Ask: “What can I understand even when it is not directly written?”

Important tip

Do not guess only from one clue. Use several clues together: character + setting + action + emotion + sequence.
